About the Website

Bootcamp for Science is dedicated to exploring the beauty and logic of physics, mathematics, and chemistry, with an emphasis on clear explanations and intuitive understanding.

The website contains lessons and resources covering a wide range of topics, including classical mechanics, calculus, electromagnetism, quantum mechanics, and more. The materials are designed to clarify difficult concepts, develop physical and mathematical intuition, and provide useful resources for students preparing for exams.

Some sections follow a structured course-like approach and focus on core topics that students commonly encounter in school and introductory university courses. Other sections are more exploratory, examining interesting problems and ideas that reveal deeper connections in mathematics and science.

For IB Students

The IB Physics curriculum combines several subjects—including one-dimensional motion, two-dimensional motion, Newton's laws, momentum, work, and energy—within the broader study of mechanics.

If you are reviewing mechanics, I recommend working through the relevant sections from motion through forces and energy rather than studying only one individual chapter. Where appropriate, individual units will also include problems written specifically in the style of IB Physics questions.

Tips for Studying

Study Before Class

Reading the relevant textbook material before class can make new concepts much easier to follow. When reading, take notes and work through some of the problems rather than reading passively.

For IB students, it can be helpful to first work through textbook problems to develop your understanding and then use IB-style questions as exam preparation.

Take Organized Notes

Take notes while reading your textbook and during class. In addition to definitions and derivations, consider keeping sections for common misconceptions, useful problem-solving methods, and representative problems.

Practice with Exam-Style Problems

Before an exam, work through problems that match the style and difficulty of the course. For IB Physics, past-paper or QuestionBank-style problems can be particularly useful. If you are unsure about a problem, compare your reasoning carefully with the mark scheme or solution.

Build a Problem Collection

Keep a collection of problems that you found difficult or answered incorrectly on homework, quizzes, and exams. Revisiting these problems is an effective way to identify recurring weaknesses and check whether you have actually mastered a topic.
