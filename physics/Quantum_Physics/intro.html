What is Quantum Physics?

We have spent a long time studying classical, or Newtonian, mechanics. We will now delve into a new field: quantum physics. Quantum physics is the study of matter and energy at the most fundamental level and of how particles interact to make up the physical world.

This course is divided into three parts. In the first part, we introduce fundamental concepts including the statistical interpretation of the wave function, the Schrödinger equation, Hermitian operators, and inner products. We also discuss wave packets, time evolution, the Ehrenfest theorem, and the uncertainty principle.

In the second part, we explore these ideas in greater depth. In the third part, we introduce three-dimensional scattering theory.

Textbooks

The two primary textbooks used in this course are Introduction to Quantum Mechanics by David J. Griffiths and A Modern Approach to Quantum Mechanics by John S. Townsend.

The books cover many of the same topics but organize and approach the material differently. Griffiths uses a somewhat more mathematically intensive approach, while Townsend provides an alternative presentation that can be useful for developing physical intuition. The course primarily follows Townsend's organization, supplemented with material from Griffiths.
